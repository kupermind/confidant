Under the hood

What one press of Prove I hold X XRP does, what each step writes on the XRP Ledger, and what the gate at the pool's door is. Nothing on this page is needed to use the app; it is here so that nothing is hidden.

What the button does, step by step

1 · Entry credential (only when your account lacks it). The pool's issuer writes an XLS-70 Credential of type ConfidantKYC to your XRPL account (CredentialCreate), and your account accepts it with its own signature (CredentialAccept). The pool's verifiers refuse a deposit — and the quorum refuses to write a proof-of-funds credential — for an account without it. In this demo the issuer's key sits in the same browser, so the app issues the credential to itself; in a real deployment a third party writes it.

2 · Prove. Your browser builds a zero-knowledge proof (the Orchard circuit, Halo 2) that unspent notes worth at least your whole private balance are yours — bound to your account, a request id, the current ledger and a 24-hour expiry. Nothing moves. The proof goes to the quorum for the next step and nowhere else.

3 · Record. Each signer's node re-verifies the proof read-only against its own state; k of N signers sign one CredentialCreate from the pool account to yours: type ConfidantPoF, URI confidant/pof/v1|X|L|E|digest, expiry 24 hours from ledger L's close time. Every signer signs only fields its own node derived.

4 · Confirm. Your account signs CredentialAccept. From here the credential counts: a lender who types your address on the lender page reads "controlled at least X XRP as of ledger L" — not "controls it now".

5 · Refresh. A private transfer of your whole balance to yourself: the notes the proof named are spent into fresh ones, so your next payment cannot be tied to the proof. Why: the proof as built reveals the identifiers (nullifiers) of the proven notes to the signers' nodes. That is the honest limit of this flow — demo-grade until a circuit that reveals none of them ships.

The receipt the app offers after the press lists exactly these transactions and nothing secret.

What the gate is, mechanically

On this testnet the issuer is an account operated by the Confidant team — the issuer is a demo; the gate on the ledger is real. The pool trusts one issuer: reading the pool's configuration…. Only credentials issued by that account count; a credential from any other account is ignored by the pool.

What is checked. One on-ledger fact: that the depositing XRPL account holds an accepted XLS-70 Credential of the configured type from the configured issuer, and that it has not expired. Nothing inside the pool is examined — not notes, not balances, not transfers.

Who checks. Each verifier node, by reading the ledger itself (ledger_entry credential) at submit and again at endorse; a signer sidecar signs nothing its own node did not accept. The issuer only writes the credential; the holder's CredentialAccept signature is what binds the account.

What the credential is. A yes/no object on the XRP Ledger from the issuer to the account, with an optional expiry; the issuer can delete it. No personal data is on the ledger or seen by the pool. What the issuer checked before writing it is the issuer's business, not the protocol's — the gate is gatekeeping on a trusted issuer's say-so at the public boundary, not a proof about any note.

Why a per-pool switch. An operator who wants to restrict who may enter configures an issuer and a type on the nodes; an operator who does not runs the pool open. The protocol is the same either way.

Team: the issuer key in this browser

This browser holds no issuer key. The app issues the entry credential by itself only when the key of the issuer the pool trusts is in this browser; without it, the Prove and Shield buttons say what is missing.

The seed stays in this browser's storage and is sent nowhere. Testnet only.
