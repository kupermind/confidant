Verify a proof of funds

A borrower can show you they control at least X XRP inside the pool without unshielding and without revealing which notes. You paste the proof package they send you; every verifier node checks it read-only against its own state and answers a verdict. What a verdict means, word for word: someone controlling at least X in this pool answered this challenge as of the validated ledger — an underwriting signal, a snapshot, not a lock, and not proof that the borrower owns the notes. Between the proof and your decision the holder can still spend.

1 · Ask the borrower

Tell the borrower what to prove and give them an id only you know. They open the app, Prove funds, type the amount, your id and their XRPL account, and send you the package the app produces.

3 · On the ledger (when the borrower recorded it)

The borrower can have the pool's quorum record the verdict as an expiring XLS-70 Credential on their XRPL account (ConfidantPoF, issued by the pool account, confirmed by the borrower's own signature). Then you need no file at all: a loan broker reads one credential. Its URI carries the threshold, the ledger window and a digest of the challenge — this page compares it with the package you verified.

What you are — and are not — told

Told. That whoever answered the challenge controlled unspent notes worth at least the amount, as of the ledger each node judged against; that the challenge named this borrower account, your id, a recent ledger and an expiry; which nodes agree, and on which state root.

Not told. The balance beyond the threshold, the borrower's history, or anything about other holders. The nodes keep no copy of the proof and log nothing; you hold the only record. Told, and worth knowing: the package carries the nullifiers of the proven notes. A nullifier does not say which note or how much, but it reappears in the public log when that note is spent, so you can tell when the borrower next spends them — not to whom or how much. Treat the package as the borrower's private data and do not pass it on. The proof could have been produced by anyone who controls the notes — binding it to the borrower's account is the challenge's job, and the borrower's signature on an XRPL Credential (the next step of this design) would tighten it.

Not a lock. The holder can spend right after proving. Treat an older verdict as older. For a lock you need collateral that moves (an escrow), which is a different construction.

Seen by the node operators. Your lender id, the borrower's account, the amount and the ledger window travel to every node a proof is posted to — the operators of those nodes see them; they are not hidden from them. On the ledger only the credential's URI carries the amount and the window; the lender is a digest there. A credential's Expiration is derived from ledger L's close time at 4 s per ledger and can outlive ledger E by minutes — "as of ledger L" is the statement, not the expiry.
