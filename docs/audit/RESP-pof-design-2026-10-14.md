# RESP — proof of shielded funds (design review of PR #29)

From the audit session, 2026-10-14, verbatim (also posted as a comment on PR #29). A design verdict
on the approach; no code is ruled mergeable — the firsthand IMPL-PASS and the mutation table come
with the slice if the owner chooses option C for the hackathon.

```text
PR #29 proof-of-funds spike: DESIGN-PASS (conditional) — the approach is cryptographically sound for an EVENT-scope underwriting SIGNAL; I read the spike firsthand (sighash.rs/verifier.rs/wallet.rs diff), it matches your description, and I agree with your read on all five, with the conditions below. Not a FAIL. Post this on #29 and record REQ/RESP; the firsthand IMPL-PASS + mutation table come at the slice if option C is chosen.

Firsthand confirmations: POF_PERSONALIZATION = b"ConfidantPoF_v1_" (exactly 16B, the BLAKE2b personal limit); pof_ctx = len(challenge) u32-LE ‖ challenge ‖ amount u64-LE (length-prefixed ⇒ no concat-ambiguity); verify_proof_of_funds is &self/read-only and runs value_balance==X(>0, exact i64) → known-anchor → nullifiers-unspent-and-pairwise-distinct → verify_proof(vk) → spend-auth sigs under pof_sighash → binding sig under pof_sighash. The PoF record is built op=Unshield/external="" and is distinguishable from a real unshield ONLY by the sighash its sigs are over — which makes one condition load-bearing (Q1-ii below).

Q-POF-1 (domain separation sufficient?): YES, mechanism sound. Conditions:
 (i) STANDING, not hoped: a CI/test guard that enumerates EVERY BLAKE2b personalization and ctx-shape in the tree and asserts "ConfidantPoF_v1_" is unique and no pool op computes it or a colliding ctx. Your "as long as no future op collides" is correct but must be ENFORCED by a glob-all guard (completeness-guard discipline — a check that samples a subset certifies a subset), because it is a time-stable claim: name the future event that breaks it (a new op reusing the personalization) and let the test fail then.
 (ii) LOAD-BEARING: confirm EVERY pool apply-path — not just submit_unshield — re-verifies BOTH the spend-auth AND the binding signature under the POOL sighash BEFORE it consumes any nullifier or applies state. The whole separation rests on this: a PoF record is a structurally-valid unshield-shaped bundle; the only thing stopping it being applied is that re-check failing. One apply-path that consumes nullifiers before (or without) the binding-sig check defeats it — for ANY foreign-sighash bundle, not just PoF. The test proves submit_unshield refuses with InvalidSpendAuth; the slice must prove it for every apply entrypoint.
 (iii) Break-test BOTH directions with a mutation per site: a PoF record POSTed as each pool op is refused, AND a genuine pool op fails verify_proof_of_funds. (You have the first direction; add the second and the per-site mutations.)
 (iv) Defense-in-depth: give the PoF record a distinct op-tag / record type so it is structurally UN-ROUTABLE to any apply-path (fail-fast before the sig-check), rather than relying on the sighash sig-check as the sole last line. Plus your (f): the UI/API must have NO path that sends a PoF record to a coordinator — "a proof of funds is not an operation."

Q-POF-2 (range ≥X): SOUND, no hole — I agree with your (b). value_balance==X exactly (i64) + X>0 + Orchard's non-negative outputs ⇒ Σinputs = X + change ≥ X; the binding sig ties Σcv_net to value_balance; ≤~15 actions ⇒ the i128 ValueSum cannot wrap. The one item-344-class pin is satisfied: `amount as i64` for amount ≥ 2^63 reinterprets to NEGATIVE (not a small positive), so no large-X-inflation-by-wrap — a real positive-funds bundle can never match a negative target, and X is additionally bound into the sighash via pof_ctx. Keep the exact-i64 compare + reject-mismatch (it is there).

Q-POF-3 (privacy): the nullifier reveal → forward spend-linkability is INHERENT to reusing the unshield bundle (verify reads the nullifiers). Self-rotation is an acceptable EVENT mitigation WITH the explicit label. Two refinements: (i) narrow WHO sees the nullifiers — if the quorum verifies and issues the XLS-70 Credential, keep the PoF record and its nullifiers OUT of the lender's view and off any public log (your (f) supports this: it is not an operation, so it need not be logged); that turns "lender can link future spends" into "only the quorum can," a strictly smaller exposure. (ii) The real fix — a circuit-level PoF that reveals no nullifier — is the post-event roadmap (out of the reuse-Orchard-unchanged scope, correctly). Acceptable for the event.

Q-POF-4 (transferability): correctly characterized — an underwriting SIGNAL, not an ownership/identity proof. The proof needs `ask`, so only the note-controller can produce it, but the note-controller ≠ necessarily the borrower (a wealthy third party can answer a borrower-bound challenge). Binding the challenge to lender-id + borrower-XRPL-account + validated ledger-index narrows it and your spike already does this (the test's `ch` = "lender-nonce|rBorrower|ledger"); it does not close it. Verdict: ACCEPTABLE as a signal with a MANDATORY label; this is intent-dependent — it becomes a BLOCKER only if the product ever presents it as "borrower B owns ≥X," which would be false. That framing line is the owner's product call; as an underwriting signal, pass.

Q-POF-5 (snapshot vs lock): correct — it is a point-in-time snapshot (verify is read-only against the CURRENT spent-set; after the proven notes are spent the proof fails DoubleSpend, confirmed in the test). A lock = quorum-enforced encumbrance = a trust-path/protocol change = OUT of scope for the event; say so. Mitigation is the right one: bind the challenge to an expiry / ledger-index window and have the lender RE-VERIFY at decision time against current state (verify already reads the live nullifier set). Make the snapshot (not guarantee) nature explicit on the artifact.

Also adopt your (a) as a condition (challenge = lender identity + borrower XRPL account + validated ledger-index/expiry; re-verify at decision) and (f) (never to a coordinator; and if option C is chosen the core slice gets coverage→100%, the mutation table on the five verify checks, wasm export, REQ/RESP). I am NOT ruling anything mergeable — this is a design verdict on the approach; the slice brings the firsthand IMPL-PASS. A peer cannot authorize what the owner must: put this to the owner as the DESIGN-PASS/conditions it is, and the owner decides option C and the ownership-claim framing (Q4).
```
